LIFE ON LAND

Protecting biodiversity. Restoring ecosystems. Securing our future.

UN Sustainable Development Goal 15 calls for protecting, restoring and promoting the sustainable use of land ecosystems: managing forests sustainably, combating land degradation and halting biodiversity loss.

Explore

Six pressures on the living land

Forests, grasslands, wetlands and mountains support wildlife, store carbon, protect soil and provide food and water. Human activity is putting them under strain.

Biodiversity loss

What: a decline in the variety of life, from genes and species to whole ecosystems.

Why: habitat loss, over-exploitation, invasive species, pollution and climate change.

Impact: less resilient ecosystems that struggle to supply clean water, pollination and healthy soil.

Deforestation

Forests are cleared for farmland, timber and infrastructure. Cleared land loses wildlife habitat, stored carbon and the soil protection that roots provide.

Habitat destruction and fragmentation

Roads, fields and buildings split habitats into small patches. Animals cannot move, feed or breed freely, and isolated populations become more vulnerable.

Land degradation

Erosion, overuse and poor management reduce the productivity of soil. Degraded land holds less water and carbon and supports less life.

Species extinction

Extinction happens when the last individual of a species dies. It is permanent, and each loss can disrupt the food webs the species belonged to.

Unsustainable agriculture

Why: expanding farmland, single-crop systems, overgrazing and heavy chemical use to meet growing demand for food.

Impact: habitat loss, tired soils and declining wildlife on and around farmland.

Solutions and conservation

Each problem has a practical response. Together they work towards SDG 15's aims of protecting, restoring and sustainably using land.

Forest conservation and reforestation

Protecting existing forest and replanting degraded land keeps habitat, carbon stores and soil in place. This supports the goal of sustainable forest management.

Protecting endangered species

Tools like the IUCN Red List show which species need action first, guiding protected areas and recovery programmes.

Sustainable farming

Methods that protect soil and make better use of inputs let farms produce food while leaving room for wildlife. UNEP highlights innovators making farming easier on the planet.

Habitat restoration

Restoring wetlands, woodland and grassland, and reconnecting fragmented patches, helps wildlife return and move.

Reducing land degradation

Healthy soil management, careful grazing and restoring damaged land bring degraded areas back into productive, living use.

International conservation efforts

Nature does not follow borders. The UN's Sustainable Development Goals and bodies such as the IUCN and FAO coordinate shared targets, data and reporting.

Life on land in Wales

Global goals are delivered locally. Wales is a small country with a rich mix of habitats.

  • Biodiversity in Wales. Ancient woodland, upland moor, peatland, wetlands, dunes and coast support species such as otters, red kites and curlews.
  • Threats. Habitat loss, intensive land use, invasive species, pollution and climate change affect Welsh habitats as they do elsewhere.
  • Nature recovery. Restoring and reconnecting habitats helps wildlife populations recover.
  • Protecting native wildlife. Safeguarding native species keeps local ecosystems working and Welsh landscapes alive.
  • Nature Recovery Action Plan 2026. The Welsh Government's plan for reversing nature's decline in Wales. Read the plan.

Protecting nature matters at both levels: global action sets the direction, and local action in places like Wales makes it real.
